I’m **Diya Saraf**,I’m a graduate student in Computer Science at the University of Southern California, where I work with Emilio Ferrara and Luca Luceri at the Information Sciences Institute (ISI). My research lies at the intersection of computational social science, network modeling, and multimodal machine learning—I study how algorithms mediate attention, trust, and influence in online environments.

I’m interested in how recommendation systems and generative models shape what people see and believe. My work combines large-scale data collection, graph analysis, and content modeling to understand the mechanics of persuasion and coordination in digital spaces.

Current projects:
• VISTA: analyzing how TikTok’s recommendation system amplifies nicotine marketing and youth-targeted content
• Telegram Coordination Graphs: mapping synchronized propaganda networks across languages and regions (npj Complexity, 2025)
• Audit Puppets: building controlled YouTube and TikTok personas to measure algorithmic bias and exposure drift
• Generative Influence: studying how large language models are used to automate and detect influence operations

Before USC, I earned my B.S. in Computer Science & Engineering (minor in Mathematics) from Santa Clara University, where I was a Clare Boothe Luce Research Fellow advised by Yuhong Liu. My undergraduate work on online trust and emoji-based engagement in misinformation campaigns received the Best Poster Award at the SCU Engineering Design Conference and was published at IEEE DPSH 2024.

I’ve also worked as a software engineering intern at Dell Technologies, contributing to both CI/CD automation and encryption infrastructure teams.

Outside research, I enjoy exploring data storytelling, the history of technology, and how people make sense of algorithms in everyday life.
Fun fact: I love listening to pop music—from Michael Jackson and Boney M to Taylor Swift. My playlists are an unpredictable mix of retro groove and modern pop.
